# Global Rules — Apply to Every Exercise Video

## Character continuity

Use the same recurring male fitness instructor in every video whenever
character/reference locking is available. Ideally use the approved Barbell
Bench Press presenter as the character reference.

### Locked presenter

A naturally handsome athletic man approximately 28–35 years old with:

- Lean muscular athletic physique
- Developed chest, shoulders, arms, back and legs
- Strong but believable proportions
- Approximately 10–15% body fat
- Natural skin texture and pores
- Short well-groomed hair
- Calm focused facial expression
- Professional fitness-instructor appearance

He should look like an experienced natural lifter rather than a competitive bodybuilder.

Avoid: cartoonishly huge muscles, extreme vascularity, artificially shredded
physique, oily bodybuilding skin, steroid-caricature proportions, plastic
AI-looking skin.

### Locked wardrobe

Essentially the same outfit in every video:

- Fitted solid-color athletic performance T-shirt
- Black or charcoal training shorts
- Clean neutral athletic shoes

No logos, writing, jewelry, watches, hats, headphones or gloves.

### Locked gym

The same premium commercial gym environment:

- Clean rubber flooring
- Professional racks and machines
- Free-weight area
- Cable machines
- Realistic gym equipment
- Professional overhead lighting
- Equipment softly visible in background
- Slight natural depth-of-field
- Authentic modern commercial-gym appearance

## Format

Every exercise should be:

- 8 seconds
- 9:16 vertical preferred
- Ultra-photorealistic live action
- One continuous shot
- Static camera — no cuts, no zoom, no camera movement
- Completely silent
- No embedded text, rep counter, exercise title, timer, logos, watermark or app UI

Each clip demonstrates ONE clean repetition or one complete exercise cycle.
The final position should closely match the opening position whenever
possible so the clip can loop repeatedly.

## Negative requirements (every video)

Do not generate:

- Extra, missing or duplicated fingers, hands or limbs
- Morphing muscles or changing facial identity
- Equipment teleportation; plates changing size; weight changing between frames
- Warped barbells or dumbbells; floating equipment
- Objects passing through the body; impossible joint motion
- Artificial slow motion, dramatic cinematic camera moves, camera cuts, speed ramps
- Text, captions, exercise titles, timers, rep counters, logos, watermarks, app interface
- Voiceover, speech, music, gym audio, sound effects

The final videos should look like real professional fitness-instruction
footage recorded specifically for a premium mobile workout application.

Priority order: correct exercise technique → consistent anatomy → realistic
equipment physics → clean mobile framing → character continuity → seamless
looping → photorealism.
