## VIDEO #2 — INCLINE DUMBBELL PRESS (`incline_dumbbell_press`)

Create an ultra-photorealistic 8-second instructional fitness video showing one technically correct Incline Dumbbell Press repetition.

Use the recurring approved male fitness instructor and the established premium commercial gym environment.

**Equipment**
- High-quality adjustable incline bench
- Two identical dumbbells
- Bench adjusted approximately 30–40 degrees. Do NOT make the bench excessively upright.

Both dumbbells must match perfectly, remain identical in size and weight, never change shape, never disappear, never merge with hands, never change weight markings, and remain physically consistent throughout the clip.

**Camera**
Static three-quarter side/front angle approximately 30–40 degrees from the athlete. Clearly show full head, both shoulders, chest, both arms, BOTH dumbbells, hands, bench, torso, hips, legs, feet. Keep both dumbbells visible even at the bottom of the repetition.

**Starting position**
Athlete is already seated securely on the incline bench. Head and upper back supported; shoulder blades gently retracted; chest naturally elevated; feet planted firmly; glutes remain on bench; dumbbells held above upper chest; palms facing mostly forward; wrists stacked and neutral; arms extended without aggressive elbow hyperextension.

**Timeline**
- 0.0–1.0 s — Hold both dumbbells steadily above the upper chest. Body remains stable.
- 1.0–3.5 s — Lower both dumbbells slowly and symmetrically toward the upper chest. Elbows travel at approximately 45–60 degrees from the torso. Maintain neutral wrists, stable shoulders, feet planted, chest elevated, smooth control.
- 3.5–4.25 s — Reach the controlled bottom position. Dumbbells near upper-chest level. Elbows should not drop excessively far below the torso. Brief pause.
- 4.25–6.75 s — Press both dumbbells upward simultaneously. The dumbbells travel slightly inward as they rise but do not collide at the top.
- 6.75–7.4 s — Return to full controlled extension.
- 7.4–8.0 s — Hold the original top position.

**Technique rules** — No dumbbells bouncing off shoulders, excessive elbow flare, wrist collapse, uneven pressing, dramatic arching, hips leaving bench, feet moving, or dumbbells touching/smashing together.

**Loop** — Opening and closing frames should closely match. The video should look natural when repeated continuously.

## VIDEO #3 — CABLE CHEST FLY (`cable_chest_fly`)

Create an ultra-photorealistic 8-second instructional fitness video showing one controlled standing cable chest fly repetition.

**Equipment**
Realistic dual adjustable cable machine. Both pulleys set approximately around mid-chest height. Two standard cable handles. Cable lines must remain visible and physically believable: stay attached, maintain consistent thickness, maintain realistic tension, never disappear, never cross impossibly through the body, never change attachment points.

**Camera**
Static three-quarter frontal angle. Frame wide enough to clearly show entire upper body, both shoulders, both arms, BOTH hands, BOTH cable handles, visible portions of both cables, torso, hips, legs, feet.

**Starting position**
Athlete stands centered between the cable columns in a comfortable slightly staggered stance. Knees softly bent; core braced; chest tall; shoulder blades controlled; arms opened outward; elbows slightly bent; hands approximately level with chest; palms facing generally forward/inward. Maintain the same slight elbow bend throughout.

**Timeline**
- 0.0–1.0 s — Hold the open starting position.
- 1.0–3.4 s — Bring both arms forward and inward in a smooth hugging arc. Do NOT turn the movement into a press.
- 3.4–4.5 s — Hands meet or nearly meet in front of the chest. Do not crash the handles together. Brief chest contraction.
- 4.5–7.2 s — Slowly allow the arms to open again under control, returning along the same curved path.
- 7.2–8.0 s — Hold the original open position.

**Technique** — Maintain slight elbow bend, stable torso, neutral spine, controlled shoulder position, natural breathing. No excessive shoulder extension, arms moving dramatically behind torso, bouncing, torso swinging, or elbows changing angle radically.

**Physics** — Cable handles, pulleys and cables remain identical throughout. No morphing cable machine. No cable passing through limbs.

## VIDEO #14 — BARBELL BACK SQUAT (`barbell_back_squat`)

Create an ultra-photorealistic 8-second instructional fitness video showing ONE technically correct Barbell Back Squat repetition. Treat this as a premium instructional movement where biomechanics and equipment stability are more important than dramatic visuals.

**Equipment**
Professional power rack, Olympic barbell, matching moderate-weight plates, safety arms/pins correctly positioned below the bottom squat position. The athlete begins already standing with the bar unracked. Do NOT show unracking or reracking.

**Bar position**
Conventional comfortable high-bar or moderate-bar back squat position across the upper traps/rear shoulder area. Do NOT position bar directly on cervical spine.

**Camera**
Static three-quarter side view, approximately 30–45 degrees, camera around hip height. Frame wide enough to show entire head, barbell, BOTH ends of bar, weight plates, torso, hips, both knees, both legs, entire feet, floor. The viewer must clearly see squat depth and knee/hip mechanics.

**Starting position**
Athlete stands upright. Feet approximately shoulder width; toes slightly turned outward; bar balanced securely; chest naturally tall; core braced; spine neutral; knees unlocked; weight distributed through full foot.

**Timeline**
- 0.0–1.0 s — STANDING START. Hold stable standing position.
- 1.0–3.6 s — DESCENT. Descend slowly and under control. Hips and knees bend together. Knees track naturally in the direction of toes. Maintain full-foot contact with floor. Torso inclines forward only as required by natural squat mechanics.
- 3.6–4.4 s — BOTTOM POSITION. Reach approximately parallel or slightly below parallel, depending on natural anatomy and mobility. Maintain stable feet, neutral controlled spine, knees aligned with toes, bar centered over mid-foot. Brief controlled pause.
- 4.4–7.2 s — ASCENT. Drive upward smoothly. Hips and shoulders rise together. Knees extend naturally. Bar remains balanced. No excessive good-morning motion.
- 7.2–8.0 s — STANDING LOOP POSITION. Return to the exact original standing posture. Hold stable.

**Technique requirements** — No knees collapsing dramatically inward, heels lifting, excessive lumbar rounding, extreme butt wink, excessive forward bar drift, hips shooting upward ahead of chest, bar bouncing, failed repetition, shaking, or extreme powerlifting stance. Use a moderate believable training weight; the athlete should clearly have several repetitions left in reserve.

**Barbell physics (critical)** — Bar remains straight; same plates throughout; equal plates on both sides; plates never disappear; bar never changes length; bar never passes through athlete; hands remain securely gripping bar; rack stays geometrically stable; safeties remain in place; floor remains stationary.

**Human anatomy** — Natural knee motion, realistic hip movement, consistent limb lengths, stable shoes, stable feet, stable hands, stable facial identity. No limb morphing, duplicated legs, extra feet, or knees bending unnaturally.

**Loop requirement** — Opening and closing standing positions should match as closely as possible. The repeated clip should appear like a continuous set (stand → squat → stand → squat → stand) without an obvious reset.
